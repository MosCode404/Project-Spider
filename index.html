ABOUT BIRD SPIDERS

What is a bird spider?

Bird spider is a common name associated with tarantulas. Tarantulas are large, hairy spiders belonging primarily to the family Theraphosidae.

They occur across tropical and subtropical regions of the world and display remarkable diversity in size, coloration, habitat and behavior.

HABITAT

Where bird spiders live

Bird spiders can be found in a wide range of environments, including tropical forests, grasslands, savannas and other warm regions.

Some species spend much of their lives on the ground, while others are adapted to living in trees and vegetation.

Their natural environment strongly influences their behavior and physical adaptations.

ANATOMY

Built for survival

Bird spiders have eight legs, two main body sections, sensory structures and specialized appendages used for feeding and interacting with their environment.

Their hairs can provide sensory information, while some New World species possess urticating hairs that can be used defensively.

BEHAVIOR

Understanding bird spider behavior

Bird spiders are generally solitary animals. Their behavior can vary considerably between species and individual animals.

Defensive displays, retreating, burrowing, web-building and changes in activity can all provide clues about their condition and environment.

LIFE CYCLE

The remarkable process of molting

Bird spiders grow by periodically shedding their old exoskeleton in a process called molting.

During and around a molt, a spider can become particularly vulnerable. It should generally be left undisturbed while molting.

RESPONSIBLE KEEPING

Caring for a bird spider

Keeping a bird spider requires research and preparation. Housing should reflect the needs of the individual species.

Important considerations include enclosure design, ventilation, substrate, hydration, temperature, feeding and security.

Care requirements differ between species, so species-specific information should always be consulted.
